# The daily rhythm — a shape, not a schedule 🔁

This is **not** an hour-by-hour timetable. Rigid schedules break on the first
bad day and then you quit. This is a *sequence* — five beats in order. The
clock times are suggestions that slide earlier as your wake time moves.

## The five beats

```
  ☀️  1. MORNING ANCHOR   →  🧠  2. ONE DEEP BLOCK  →  🏃  3. MOVE
                                                            ↓
              🌙  5. WIND DOWN   ←   🔵  4. ONE ADMIN/ERRAND
```

### 1. ☀️ Morning anchor (first 30 min up)
Curtains open → light in eyes 10 min → water → feet on floor. No phone doom-
scroll, no email yet. This is the keystone; protect it above everything.

### 2. 🧠 One deep-work block (~90 min, mid-morning)
**Pick ONE project** from `backlog.md` (🟣 section). Not five. One. Do its
*next action*. Phone in another room. A messy 25 minutes counts — starting is
the whole game.

### 3. 🏃 Move your body (early afternoon — before the "too late" story starts)
The bar is *leaving the house*, not a perfect workout. Menu, easiest → hardest:
- 10-min walk in daylight (this alone counts — double it as light + errand)
- Run
- Climbing session
Pick whatever you'll actually do today. **Motion is the mood-fixer** — do it
even when (especially when) you don't feel like it.

### 4. 🔵 One admin/errand (afternoon)
One thing off the 🔵 batch in `backlog.md`. One call, or one errand. Pair
errands with beat 3 (the Whole Foods return *is* leaving the house).

### 5. 🌙 Wind down (~1 hr before "in bed by")
Dim the lights, screens down or on night mode, curtains/lights low. Tomorrow's
top-1 written down so your brain can let go of it. This is how the early wake
time becomes sustainable — the evening makes the morning.

## The two survival rules

- **Bad day? Shrink, don't skip.** Only got the morning anchor? That's still a
  win — you kept the keystone. Only a 10-min walk instead of a run? Win.
- **"It's too late" is a lie.** Late light > no light. Late walk > no walk.
  The door opens at any hour.

## Why this works (the one-paragraph science)

Your body clock is set primarily by **light timing**, not willpower. Bright
light in your eyes shortly after waking tells your brain "day started now,"
which pulls your sleepiness earlier that night, which makes the next early
wake easier. Consistent wake time + morning light is the entire mechanism. The
curtains are not a metaphor — they're the intervention.
